Support at Home

Psychosocial support for older people... navigating ageing, illness and loss. Delivered under clinical supports, which means no contribution from you.

For you and your family

The part nobody schedules

Your package covers a great deal. Someone to help with the house. Nurses for the wound, the medication, the equipment. All of it necessary, all of it practical.

What it doesn't cover is the adjustment itself. A body that has changed and didn't ask your permission. Relationships finding a new shape. A diagnosis you are still absorbing, months after everyone else has moved on from the news.

That is what these sessions are for. Not to be brave in. Just to be honest in.

There is no cost to you. Counselling sits under clinical supports, which carry no participant contribution, so your sessions are fully funded under your package.

How it happens

In your own home, wherever you are most comfortable, or by video if you would rather not have another person at the door. Fifty minutes. As often as suits you and your package.

Family can be part of it if you want them there, and not if you don't. Nothing you say gets passed on without your say so.

In-home visits beyond the Hervey Bay city area by arrangement.

For care partners, coordinators and clinicians

The counsellor's role in participant care

Practical supports keep the household running and clinical services treat the condition. What remains is the adjustment itself: a changed body, relationships finding new shape, a diagnosis still being absorbed. This is where counselling works, in partnership with the care team.

The work follows the participant's own goals and takes on the barriers that are not physical: confidence, tolerance of distress, and authority over decisions about their own care.

The approach

Counselling is evidence-informed, drawing on acceptance and commitment therapy and on meaning-centred and dignity-informed approaches developed for living alongside chronic and serious illness, integrated with nervous system regulation, breathwork and therapeutic movement where clinically appropriate.

A background in neuroscience and pathology (BMedSci) means sessions address both the cognitive and physiological dimensions of adjustment, meeting the participant where they are that day.

Who to refer

Older people living with chronic, serious or life-limiting illness, functional decline or bereavement.

  • Difficulty adjusting to diagnosis or prognosis
  • Confidence not restored after recovery
  • Fear-driven avoidance and reduced activity
  • Withdrawal from personal care, contact or community
  • Refusal of care, equipment or assistance
  • Rehabilitation stalled without physical explanation
  • Anticipatory grief and end-of-life adjustment
  • Loss of identity, role, licence or home
No diagnosis needed to refer. Suitability requires capacity for reflective conversation. Not indicated for acute presentations or advanced dementia. Where needs fall beyond counselling scope, a referral will be advised.
