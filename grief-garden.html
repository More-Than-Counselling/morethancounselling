Grief lives in the body

It shows up as tiredness, heaviness in the chest, a foggy mind or a restless heart. It often needs a kind of care that words alone can't reach.

This two-hour session offers a quiet, supportive space to tend your grief through gentle yoga, breathwork, restorative rest and reflection. Whatever your loss, and however long ago, you are welcome here.

  • No yoga experience is needed
  • There is no pressure to talk or share

The parts no one tells you

Grief takes something you didn't know you had. A kind of innocence... the quiet belief that this happens to other people, not to you. That the people you love will still be here tomorrow.

It wakes you in the night, because for just a moment you forgot that loss was part of your world. Then you remember, and it comes crashing in again, the shock and trauma felt all over again in every cell of your body.

It's reaching for your phone to tell them something. A song in the supermarket that stops you in the aisle. The world carrying on as normal while yours has quietly rearranged itself.

None of this means you're doing grief wrong. It means you loved.

Grief is love that has lost its usual place to go.

What grief does in the body

Grief isn't only something we feel. It's something the whole body goes through. Part of the morning is a short, gentle teaching, in plain language, on:

  • Why loss can register in the nervous system as threat, leaving you exhausted, foggy or on edge
  • How the mind, nervous system and immune system talk to each other, and why grief can show up as aches and illness
  • Why grief comes in waves, moving between the loss and the rest of life, and why both are healthy
  • Why the bond with the person you love doesn't have to end for you to heal
  • How breath and gentle movement send the body signals of safety that words can't

How the morning unfolds

Arriving

A gentle welcome, slow breath and time to settle into the space.

Moving

Slow, floor-based movement through the hips and body, for what feels heavy, with room for tears or stillness.

Understanding

A short, gentle teaching on what grief does in the body and nervous system, and why breath and movement help.

Reflecting

Quiet time to write in your own notebook, with gentle prompts. Nothing is shared unless you choose to.

Resting

Calming breathwork, then yoga nidra or guided meditation in deeply supported rest.

Closing

One small intention, written down, to carry into the week ahead.
